# yc-outreach — Competitive Positioning, User Research, Technical Architecture Strategy (2026-10-23)

Scope note: a parallel workflow owns `PLAN-2026-10-23-DEEP.md` (pages/APIs/features) for this
product; it did not exist at write time. This doc covers positioning, personas, journey, and
growth only — no feature/page/API spec duplication.

What the repo actually does today (read from `README.md`, `index.html`, `api/yc.py`,
`yc_scraper.py`, `apify_enrich.py`):
- Single static page (`index.html`, no framework/build) + one Python serverless function
  (`api/yc.py`) deployed free on Vercel. No account, no database; all state (name, template,
  "sent" marks, loaded batches) lives in `localStorage`.
- Lets a user pick a YC batch, loads 20 companies at a time, pulls each founder's name, title,
  LinkedIn/X, and the company's open YC job postings (title + link) straight from YC's own
  `ycombinator.com/companies/<slug>` page data.
- Resolves an email per founder in priority order: Apify-verified (user's own token, client-side
  only) > Apify-unverified > scraped from the company site (including decoding Cloudflare's
  `data-cfemail` obfuscation) > pattern guess (`first@domain`, etc. — 80% first-guess accuracy on
  the README's own 151-email benchmark).
- One editable template with `{first_name} {company} {one_liner} {batch} {my_name} {portfolio}
  {github} {resume}` placeholders; copy-to-clipboard per founder, mark as sent, CSV export.
- Already forked 6+ times on GitHub under different usernames (wenayy, lakshh07, moizghumann,
  adityajha2005 — this repo's own `origin` — DevItaliya22, udayempire), i.e. it's an OSS template
  that already spreads organically; one fork (`lakshh07/yc-outreach`) is a CLI variant that sends
  directly from the user's Gmail instead of requiring copy-paste.

## 1. Direct competitors

- **Work at a Startup** (workatastartup.com, YC's own official job board) — Free.
  - Strongest feature: every YC company is on it; founders see your profile directly, zero spam
    stigma, the default channel job seekers already trust.
  - Weakest point: purely passive — you wait to be found; founders read roughly 5% of cold
    inbound applications on it (cited by standout.work), no outreach tooling, no email finding.
- **Chiaro** (chiaroai.com, iOS app) — subscription/freemium, price not publicly listed.
  - Strongest feature: swipe-based discovery + "Autopilot" that auto-sends a hyper-personalized
    email to the founder instantly; targets $150K-$350K+ founding-engineer roles at YC/a16z
    startups.
  - Weakest point: iOS-only, auto-send is a black box (little control over exact wording sent),
    no bulk filtering by hiring status/industry/batch.
- **Backdoor AI** (backdoorai.app) — subscription, price not publicly listed.
  - Strongest feature: full-funnel tracking with published stats (9 of 10 users who get a reply
    hear back within two weeks; 4 of 10 founder replies come after a follow-up it sent); keeps a
    human-approval step before sending.
  - Weakest point: closed SaaS, account required, no way to audit how it finds/verifies emails;
    recurring subscription for something this repo does for free.
- **VCBacked.co** — Free tier (5 verified leads/week); paid $79/mo or $299/yr.
  - Strongest feature: verified founder emails across 6,302+ YC companies spanning 48 batches,
    one-click bulk CSV export.
  - Weakest point: paywalls exactly the data (verified emails) a job seeker needs most; built for
    sales/recruiting/investor deal-sourcing, not job search — no resume/portfolio personalization,
    no per-founder open-role matching.
- **Plox YC Founder List** (tryplox.com) — Free CSV, 8,000+ founders.
  - Strongest feature: zero cost, drops straight into Clay/Apollo/Instantly.
  - Weakest point: static snapshot that goes stale as batches turn over; no live hiring-role data,
    no template/personalization/send workflow — just a raw list.
- **Origami.chat** — Starter $29/mo (2,000 credits) up to Max $499/mo.
  - Strongest feature: built-in multi-channel sequencer (LinkedIn + email, up to 10 steps each,
    unlimited sends on paid plans, no per-seat fee).
  - Weakest point: general-purpose prospecting tool retrofitted with YC-themed blog content, not
    YC-native; credit-metered and overpriced for someone outreaching to a single ~100-230-company
    batch.
- **Apollo.io / Hunter.io / Clay** (generic B2B contact/outreach platforms) — Apollo $49-119/
  user/mo, Hunter $34-349/mo, Clay $185-495/mo.
  - Strongest feature: huge general contact databases plus mature sequencing/CRM infrastructure.
  - Weakest point: zero YC-awareness (no batch/industry/hiring/job-title filters, no one-liner
    context); per-seat/per-credit pricing is overkill for a single-batch job search.
- **lakshh07/yc-outreach** (sibling OSS fork, CLI) — Free.
  - Strongest feature: sends the email directly from the user's own Gmail — no copy-paste step.
  - Weakest point: CLI only, no live search/filter UI, no on-site email scraping or Cloudflare
    de-obfuscation, no cross-session "sent" tracking or CSV export.

## 2. Differentiation plan (concrete, per competitor)

- **vs VCBacked ($79/mo) and Apollo/Hunter/Clay (per-seat/per-credit)**: stay fully free, no
  signup, no card — this is already true today; the move is to keep it that way as the explicit
  wedge against the exact thing VCBacked paywalls (verified founder emails) and the per-seat fees
  the generic tools charge.
- **vs Chiaro/Backdoor AI (app download + account + subscription)**: keep the zero-install,
  zero-account single-page flow — a user goes from opening the URL to a copyable personalized
  email in under two minutes, with no onboarding screen, no app store install, no login.
- **vs VCBacked/Plox/Origami/Apollo (none surface open roles per founder)**: this repo already
  shows each founder's actual open job postings (title + link) next to their email — the one
  feature that lets a pitch say "saw you're hiring a {job_title}" instead of a generic line. None
  of the list/database competitors expose this; Chiaro/Backdoor do, but only behind a paid app.
  Lean harder into this by surfacing job title directly inside the template placeholders (it is
  not currently one of the eight listed vars) so every generated email can reference the specific
  open role automatically.
- **vs VCBacked/Plox/Apollo/Hunter pattern-guessing (none special-case site obfuscation)**: this
  repo already decodes Cloudflare's `data-cfemail` obfuscation (README notes ~1-in-16 sites use
  it) to recover real published addresses that competitors' pattern-matching misses — a concrete,
  already-built technical edge to market explicitly rather than bury in a changelog line.
- **vs lakshh07's Gmail-send CLI fork (the one thing the sibling fork has that this repo lacks)**:
  port a client-side Gmail send step into `index.html` (OAuth to the user's own Gmail, same "be
  decent" daily cap/delay the CLI fork already does) so copy-paste disappears entirely — this
  closes the only functional gap inside the same OSS family while staying no-account like the
  rest of the page.
- **vs Work at a Startup's passive queue (5% founder read rate per standout.work)**: market the
  tool explicitly against this number — "founders read ~5% of inbound on Work at a Startup; this
  reaches the other 95% directly" — a named, quantified contrast rather than a vague "better"
  claim.
- **vs Backdoor AI/Chiaro's closed black-box send**: stay open source and keep the Apify token
  flow client-side-only (already true) — the explicit trust pitch for a technical audience that
  can read the code and verify no data leaves the browser except their own optional Apify call.

## 3. Primary user persona / ICP

- **Primary**: individual software engineers (new-grad through senior, especially candidates
  chasing "founding engineer" roles) who want to skip the standard ATS/Work-at-a-Startup queue and
  email YC founders directly. The template's own placeholder set — `{portfolio} {github}
  {resume}` — is designed around this persona, not a sales rep or recruiter.
- **Secondary**: recruiters/agencies and BD/sales reps prospecting YC companies (the industry and
  "hiring" filters serve this group too), but the resume/github/portfolio fields confirm the
  primary design target is the individual engineer pitching themselves, not a company pitching a
  service.
- **Job-to-be-done**: "Give me a short, current list of YC companies that are actually hiring for
  my skill set right now, with the founder's real email and their specific open role, so I can
  send one honest, personalized email instead of mass-applying into Work at a Startup's queue
  where only ~5% of inbound gets read."

## 4. User journey map (discovery -> paying/retained)

1. **Discovery** — finds the tool via GitHub (already forked 6+ times under different usernames,
   i.e. organic template/search virality) or via a blog post/Reddit/HN thread about emailing YC
   founders for a job.
2. **Landing** — opens the Vercel-deployed page (or runs `python3 serve.py` locally); no signup
   wall, reads the one-paragraph description at the top.
3. **Setup** — fills in name/portfolio/github/resume and lightly edits the default subject/body
   template; everything saved to `localStorage`.
4. **Target discovery** — picks a batch, toggles "hiring" and/or an industry filter, clicks "Load
   companies"/"Load more" repeatedly (20 companies per click, ~5-10s each).
5. **Evaluation** — scans founders for high-reliability email badges and roles matching their
   skills; optionally pastes an Apify token to upgrade guesses to verified emails.
6. **Action** — copies (today manually; see differentiation plan) the personalized email per
   founder and marks each company as "sent" to track progress.
7. **Retention** — the natural return trigger is YC's batch cadence (new batch roughly every six
   months, or whenever "Load more" surfaces fresh postings within an open batch) — this is
   inherently a recurring, cadence-driven tool rather than a one-time-use one.
8. **Monetization** — none today; fully free and open source. The only money that moves is the
   user's own optional, capped Apify spend (default $1/run), which this project never touches or
   collects.

**Biggest drop-off risk step**: step 5, the optional Apify-token decision. Most engineers job
searching won't have an Apify account or know what Apify is, so they default to "guess" emails
(only 80% first-guess accuracy per the README's own 151-email benchmark). That's exactly where
emails bounce or go to the wrong inbox, the user's few outbound emails get no replies, trust in
the tool collapses, and they abandon before ever sending anything — a silent failure mode with no
feedback loop to tell the user *why* it didn't work.

Second-largest drop-off: step 6, the pure-friction copy-paste-into-mail-client step with no
auto-send, which the sibling `lakshh07` CLI fork already solves but this page does not.

## 5. Three concrete growth moves (follow directly from 1-4)

1. **Ship direct-send to close the step-6 drop-off and the only gap vs. the sibling CLI fork.**
   Port `lakshh07/yc-outreach`'s Gmail-send logic into `index.html` as a client-side OAuth flow to
   the user's own Gmail, with the same "be decent" daily cap/delay already documented in this
   repo's README footer. This turns the product from "list + template" into a complete send loop
   without adding an account or a backend, which is the single highest-leverage fix identified in
   the journey map's top drop-off risk area (steps 5-6 combined).
2. **Consolidate fork-traffic discovery instead of fragmenting it.** Open a PR/README note on the
   six existing GitHub forks (wenayy, lakshh07, moizghumann, DevItaliya22, udayempire, and this
   repo's own `adityajha2005` origin) cross-linking to whichever fork stays most actively
   maintained, then post a "Show HN" / r/cscareerquestions / r/ExperiencedDevs thread framed around
   the named, quantified pain point from section 2: "Work at a Startup gets founders to read ~5%
   of inbound — this reaches the other 95% directly, with their actual open role in the email."
   This targets an audience (engineers already job-hunting) that is already searching exactly this
   problem, per the "How to Find & Contact YC Founders" and "get a job at a YC startup" content
   already ranking for it.
3. **Add a no-account, no-backend "new batch" notification** (browser push permission or a static
   RSS/webhook feed generated by the existing `api/yc.py` batches endpoint) that fires when YC
   opens a new batch's hiring postings. The journey map shows the product's only retention cadence
   is YC's own batch cycle (~every six months); without a push mechanism, a user who used the tool
   once during one batch has zero reason to come back on their own for the next one. This closes
   that retention gap while staying consistent with the product's existing all-client-side, no
   backend-state design (the notification target list itself can live in `localStorage`, same as
   everything else).
